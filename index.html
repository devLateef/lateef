A record of leadership

Leadership is not a title. It is how you show up when people need you.

01. Choosing resolution over punishment

As Senior Prefect in secondary school, I represented approximately 130 students. On one occasion, some students had spoken disrespectfully about a teacher in his absence. When the matter came to light, the teacher was determined to have them expelled.

Rather than allow the situation to escalate, I intervened. I accompanied the students to meet the teacher, encouraged them to acknowledge their wrongdoing, and helped facilitate an apology. After the intervention, the proposed expulsion was reconsidered and replaced with a one-week suspension. The students returned to school the following week.

I learned that leadership sometimes means holding people accountable while also creating room for understanding, reconciliation, and a second chance.

02. Standing up when over 100 students needed someone to act

As class governor at university, I once faced a difficult situation. Our professor threatened to deduct huge penalty from the examination scores of over 100 students because we had missed a rescheduled class. The information had not reached me in time because the person carrying the message was caught in traffic and had a dead phone.

My colleagues were understandably worried. I knew I had to act. I reached out to the professor, explained the communication breakdown, and negotiated for the class to be held over the weekend. We found a way forward without the students facing the threatened penalty.

That experience reinforced my belief that representation means more than having a voice. It means using that voice to engage, negotiate, and pursue solutions.

These experiences shaped the leadership I believe in: listening before judging, advocating when it matters, bringing people together, and following through.

That is the approach I want to bring to the CMU-Africa Student Guild: practical leadership, meaningful representation, and accountability.

Accountability in action

Don't just hear promises. Track the work.

A Guild Progress Tracker would complement existing town halls and financial reporting—not replace them. It would make the follow-through on Guild commitments easier to see.

Know what happens after a concern is raised.

The proposed tracker will show what the Guild committed to, what action has been taken, the current status, and the next step. Updates will be honest and mindful of student privacy.
